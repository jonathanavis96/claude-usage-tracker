## A. The five-hour change on the meters

**Method** (`credits.five_hour_on_meters`, published as `credits.five_hour_on_meters`).
Windows per week is five-hour meter movement over seven-day meter movement. For each announced
five-hour candidate, each account's windows per week is compared either side of the candidate
instant. The readings are the account's own `weekly_windows.max20.by_window` rows, pooled and
bounded exactly as the 14 September paired measurement (#90, `_side`) pools them.

- The before side runs from the previous boundary (the 14 September weekly change or an earlier
  candidate) to the instant. Where the account's own certified weekly step starts later, the
  before side starts there instead.
- The after side runs from five hours after the instant to the next boundary. A window that
  ends within five hours of the instant began before it, so it is counted as `n_straddling`
  and used on neither side.
- An account needs 5 readings before, 1 after, and bounded rounding intervals on both sides.
- The accounts are combined the way #90 combines them (`combine_log_ratios`): a mean of log
  ratios weighted by the inverse square of each account's log half-width, with the interval
  taken as the same weighted mean of the ends.
- Five-hour change = 1 / ratio - 1, and the interval ends are transformed the same way.

This rests on the announced scope. The recorded announcement
(`credits.ANNOUNCEMENTS[1]`: "We're also raising Pro and Max users' 5-hour limit by 20%",
Anthropic email to Max subscribers, dated by MacRumors 2026-09-22) raises the five-hour limit
and leaves the weekly cap alone. With the weekly budget fixed, windows per week moves inversely
with the window. The method text says this. A candidate with no five-hour announcement
attached is not measured this way, because the ratio alone cannot say which budget moved.

**States.** The states and thresholds are the ones the credits test already uses:
`measuring` below 5 readings after on every combined account, `provisional` at 5 to 9, and
`measured` at 10 or more on at least one account. A candidate that is measured and dated after
14 September has `applies: true`. It then opens a window regime and enters `events` and
`last_change`.

The credits test also required the interval to exclude no change. That gate does not apply
here. The announcement records that the change happened, and the meters measure its size. The
meter interval is a far-ends rounding bound: the lowest after reading over the highest before,
and the other way round. With 8 to 23 windows after the change, that bound is wide. The
`interval_excludes_no_change` field is published and is false.

**Candidate instant.** The candidate instant is now the family's first turn, not the start of
its first stretch. `tracker/join.py` records each model's earliest turn per stretch
(`first_turns`), and `tracker/gs_passive.py` writes it into every stretch record.
`change_candidates` uses the earliest stamp and falls back to the stretch start where a record
has no stamp. `at_source` says which one was used. `first_seen_stretch_end` is unchanged: it is
still the end of the stretch that holds the first turn.

  2026-09-23T12:21:55Z (a2).

## Where +41% came from

The credits test values each accepted stretch in credits (`gs_passive.stretch_credits`) and
compares credits per 1% of the five-hour meter either side of the candidate, account by
account.

- In the live file (13:30Z), the candidate was 2026-09-21T17:44:09Z. The before side runs from
  14 September, and the after side is every accepted stretch that started after the instant:
  - a1: +50.6% (41 before, 10 after)
  - a2: +27.8% (40 before, 15 after)
  - a3: -7.2% (52 before, **1** after)
  - Combined with inverse-variance weights 0.66, 0.31 and 0.03: **+40.9%**, interval +20.1% to
    +65.2%.
- On this checkout's inputs the same test reads +39.5% at 21 September. At the corrected
  instant, 22 September 17:03:48Z, it reads +35.9% (a1 +36.7% on 45/9, a2 +34.5% on 41/14),
  interval +13.9% to +62.1%.
- Every account moved to Opus 5.5 on 22-23 September, so the after-side stretches are mostly
  Opus 5.5. They are valued at the measured Opus 5.5 rate from `history/model-rates.json`:
  0.776x Opus (input 0.5176 credits per token against the anchor's 0.6667). That rate has an
  80% bootstrap interval of 0.708x to 0.857x, fitted from 17 of the pooled fit's 190
  stretches. Every Opus 5.5 stretch is after the change, so that rate and any window change
  are confounded. The code already says this in `stretch_credits`.
- If only the Opus 5.5 tokens are revalued, with every other family held at its rate, the
  change at the 22 September instant reads:

| Opus 5.5 rate (x Opus) | credits-test change | interval |
|---|---|---|
| 0.708 (80% low end) | +28.4% | +7.6% to +53.1% |
| 0.776 (fitted) | +35.9% | +13.9% to +62.1% |
| 0.857 (80% high end) | +44.6% | +21.2% to +72.4% |
| 0.621 | +18.8% | -0.4% to +41.7% |
| 0.582 | +14.4% | -4.1% to +36.6% |
| 0.543 | +10.0% | -7.9% to +31.3% |
| 0.505 | +5.5% | -11.7% to +26.0% |

  The credits test agrees with the meters (+7%) only if Opus 5.5 costs about 0.52x Opus. That
  is below the fit's interval. The stretches cannot separate "the window grew 41%" from "Opus
  5.5 is cheaper than the fit says". The meters can: windows per week fell about 7%, not the
  29% that a real +41% would need.

The consequence on the page: tokens per week read 604M x 4.86 = 2.9B, back at the pre-14
September level. On the meters the current window is 461M and the week is 2.18B, level with
the 14 to 22 September regime, as a five-hour-only change under an unchanged weekly cap
should leave it (below).

## B and C. Per-regime figures

The regime boundaries are the window's own `regimes`: the 14 September weekly change plus
every five-hour change that applies. That is the union of the weekly and five-hour change
instants. For windows per week, the weekly change reaches each account at its own certified
step: a1 from 11 September, a2 from 14 September. `credits.window_tokens` now publishes:

- `regimes` (existing shape): values from part A.
- `per_week_regimes`: `{from, until, value, interval, window, windows_per_week}`, plus
  `windows_per_week_interval`, `windows_per_week_source`, `windows_per_week_pooled`,
  `windows_per_week_pooled_interval` and `n_windows_per_week`. `value` = `window` x
  `windows_per_week`, and the interval multiplies the two intervals' ends.
  - Up to the weekly change's regime, windows per week is pooled over every account's
    readings that ended in the regime (`pooled_all_accounts`).
  - A regime opened by a five-hour change chains, the way the window regimes chain: the
    previous regime's windows per week times the combined paired ratio from
    `five_hour_on_meters` (`windows_per_week_ratio`), each interval end times the ratio
    interval's same end (`previous_regime_times_paired_ratio`). Pooling there compares
    different accounts either side: a4 has readings only after the change, at 3.94, and a3
    has 2. That gave a spurious 11% drop. The pooled figure stays beside it as
    `windows_per_week_pooled`, for reference.
- `per_week` (the hero, including every `per_family.*`) now equals the newest
  `per_week_regimes` figure: the current window times the newest regime's windows per week,
  with `windows_per_week.source` naming it. Before, it was the current window times
  `weekly_windows.max20.current`, pooled since 14 September. A file whose newest regime has no
  windows per week of its own publishes a null week with a status.
- `account_regimes`: `{aN: [{from, until, window, windows_per_week, per_week, n_window,
  n_wpw}]}`. Each factor comes from that account's own readings in that regime, and is null
  where the account has none. `window` uses the site #106 conversion: the account's median
  credits per 1% (its stretches wholly inside the regime, valued as
  `five_hour_window_across_cut` values them) x 100 / that regime's pooled window credits
  (`window_credits.regimes`) x that regime's window tokens.
- `per_regime_method`: the rule in words.

A file with no five-hour change still publishes all three, over the two regimes the 14
September split gives.

## D. masterrig's passive job catches up after sleep

`bin/passive.sh` is now safe to run every hour:

- It exits 0 at once while `.passive-last-ok` in the checkout (gitignored) is under 20 hours
  old. `--force` skips the check.
- The stamp is touched only after a commit that pushed (directly, or after the existing rebase
  retry), or after a run with nothing to commit.
- A failed step (`set -e`) or a failed push leaves the stamp alone, so the next hourly tick
  tries again.

The new masterrig cron line, `15 * * * *`, is documented in `docs/deploy-gs.md`. No crontab was
edited; the director installs it. Each run is at least 20 hours after the last, so the run time
moves about four hours earlier each day, and it still runs at least once a day.
`tracker/publish.py`'s masterrig feed allowance (36 hours) still allows one late run.

Tests (`tests/test_deploy_scripts.py`, `TestPassiveShGuard`) run the real script against a
scratch clone with a bare origin and stub `tracker` modules:

- a missing stamp runs, pushes and writes the stamp;
- a fresh stamp (1 hour) skips the run;
- a stale stamp (21 hours) runs;
- `--force` runs despite a fresh stamp;
- a failed step leaves no stamp;
- a failed push leaves no stamp.

The hero's "Last sample" is `meter_read_at`, the newest gs meter reading at publish time. The
meter timers (`deploy/systemd/claude-usage-meter-*.timer`) sample every minute
(`OnUnitActiveSec=1min`), so the figure moves only when `bin/daily.sh` publishes. gs's crontab
has `30 * * * *`, so today it refreshes hourly.

**The limits, as fetched on 2026-09-28:**
