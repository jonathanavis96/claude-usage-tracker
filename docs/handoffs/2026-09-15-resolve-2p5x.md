# Resolve the 2.5x disagreement between probe and idle-burst, without generating traffic

You are working in `~/code/claude-usage-tracker` on masterrig. Read `AGENTS.md` first.

## The problem

Two independent methods measure the same quantity — how much list-price value one
percent of the five-hour usage meter buys — and disagree by a constant factor of
about 2.5 for the same model in the same era.

- **Probe** (`tracker/probe.py`, rows in `history/probes.jsonl`): sends known prompts,
  waits 60s for the meter to settle, reads the movement. Fable reads **$1.006 per 1%**.
- **Idle-bounded burst** (`tools/idle_burst.py`, added 2026-09-15): takes stretches of
  ordinary usage with 45+ minutes of silence either side, pads 10 min before and 20 min
  after, and measures total tokens against total meter movement. Fable reads
  **$2.535 per 1%** over 21 windows.

Both use the same pricing function and the same `data/prices.json` weights.

Your job is to determine **which number is correct and why the other one is wrong.**

## Hard constraint

**Do not generate traffic to measure anything.** No new probe run, no controlled burst,
no synthetic workload on any account. The answer must come from data that already exists
on disk. This is the whole point of the task — assume every measurement you need has
already been taken and is sitting in a log somewhere.

## What has already been ruled out — do not redo these

- **Not a class weight.** Setting `class_weight.cache_read` to zero still leaves $1.91;
  setting `class_weight.output` to zero still leaves $1.28. No single class absorbs a flat
  2.5x. The burst mix per 1% is output $1.31, cache_write $0.73, cache_read $0.55,
  input $0.005.
- **Not the five-hour meter decaying as a rolling window.** A 1-hour burst and a 5-hour
  burst both read ~$2.6, so duration does not drive the discrepancy.
- **Not unlogged traffic on the account.** Usage the meter charges for but the transcripts
  do not record would push the burst number *down*, not up.
- **Not solvable by regression over sampling intervals.** A non-negative least squares fit
  of token classes against per-interval meter movement over 1225 intervals returned
  R² = -0.377 — worse than predicting the mean — and was negative on every subset tried
  (delta≥3, delta≥5, Opus only, Fable only, known-model delta≥2). Cause is meter update
  lag smearing effect into the interval after the cause. Do not spend time re-deriving this.
- **Both sides are internally corroborated.** Two probe mixes with a ~700x difference in
  output share agree with each other ($1.006 cache-write-heavy, $1.004 output-heavy). The
  burst side has 21 readings clustered in $2.35–$2.99. So the error is systematic in one
  *pipeline*, not noise, and the numbers within each pipeline are consistent.
